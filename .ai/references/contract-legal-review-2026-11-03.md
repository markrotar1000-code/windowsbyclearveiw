## Material gaps in the current application

1. **Washington contractor disclosure receipt.** The current quote header has the registration ID but the application does not include the statutory Notice to Customer, a signed receipt for that notice, or a separate retention record. RCW 18.27.114 requires notice before work on covered residential projects of $1,000 or more and commercial projects of $1,000 to less than $60,000; the residential provision covers four or fewer units and accessory structures. Retain the signed disclosure for at least three years. The statute ties compliance to lien rights. Verify the current registration and bond information before completing the official form. Contractor-to-contractor/public-work exceptions require classification; do not infer applicability solely from a homeowner label.

## Proposed wording for counsel review — not published contract terms

**Scope and changes.** Contractor will perform the work and supply the materials expressly identified in this agreement. Any change to the scope, materials, contract price, or completion schedule must be described in a written change order approved by both parties before the changed work is performed. An approved change order becomes part of this agreement.

**Completion and payment.** Use the implemented payment clause above. Define project-specific completion deliverables in the scope. If the business wants payment at substantial completion despite remaining punch-list items, counsel and the owner must explicitly define and approve that different trigger; the application currently says completion of the agreed work.

**Concealed conditions and delays.** Contractor will promptly notify Customer of concealed conditions affecting the work, including rot or structural damage not reasonably observable before work begins. Additional work and any related price or schedule adjustment require an approved written change order. Completion dates are subject to reasonable extensions for weather, material shortages, or other circumstances beyond Contractor's reasonable control. Contractor will notify Customer of a material delay and the anticipated schedule adjustment.

This replaces the existing broad assertion of no responsibility with a defined extension/notice procedure. Counsel should confirm allocation of permitting, inspection, hazardous-material and code-compliance responsibilities against actual operations before adding them.

**Manufacturer warranties.** Any manufacturer's warranty is governed exclusively by that manufacturer's written terms, coverage, exclusions, and claim procedures. Customer is responsible for submitting and pursuing claims directly with the manufacturer. ClearView does not administer or assist with manufacturer claims and does not extend or replace a manufacturer's warranty. Nothing in this paragraph limits rights or remedies that applicable law does not permit the parties to waive.

The final sentence is a preservation of mandatory rights, not a new voluntary workmanship warranty. Counsel should review any intended disclaimer of implied warranties separately; the absence of a voluntary warranty should not be presented as immunity from defective-work claims.

**Governing law and venue.** This agreement is governed by Washington law. Subject to mandatory jurisdiction and venue requirements, any court proceeding arising from this agreement must be brought in a court of competent jurisdiction serving Clark County, Washington. Nothing in this agreement waives any nonwaivable statutory right or remedy.

**Acceptance.** Customer accepts the described scope, contract price, payment terms, and incorporated written change orders. Provide and separately acknowledge the statutory notices that apply to the transaction. Do not use a generic statement that a customer received forms unless the system actually supplies and records them.
